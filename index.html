Standard Operating Procedure: IRMS Peak Integration & UK Safety

⚠️

Mandatory UK Health & Safety, COSHH Risk Assessment & PPE Notice

COSHH & PPE: Verified ✓

Statutory Framework: All isotope ratio mass spectrometry operations are governed by the UK Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HSWA), the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH), and DSEAR (Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002) as enforced by the UK Health and Safety Executive (HSE).

Pre-Acquisition Risk Assessment (RA): Before opening any mass spectrometer reference gas isolation valves or starting an acquisition sequence, operators must verify that a signed Departmental Risk Assessment and COSHH Assessment are in place covering:

  • High-Pressure Compressed Gas Cylinders: Carrier (He) and reference gases (CO₂, N₂, O₂) regulated under BCGA Code of Practice CP4.
  • High-Vacuum & High-Voltage Assemblies: Turbo-molecular vacuum pumps and ion source acceleration plates operating at up to 3–10 kV.
  • Cryogenic Liquid Nitrogen (LN₂) Cold Traps: Asphyxiation, oxygen-depletion, and severe cold-burn hazards during trap maintenance.
Mandatory UK Standard PPE Checklist (All Operators Must Wear):
  • • Eye Protection (BS EN 166): Safety spectacles with lateral side shields (or chemical/cryogenic splash goggles where specified).
  • • Protective Lab Coat (ISO 13688 / BS EN 14126): Full-length Howie-style or barrier laboratory coat, fully buttoned with elasticated cuffs.
  • • Hand Protection (BS EN ISO 374 & BS EN 511): Nitrile disposable gloves for sample vial handling and insulated cryogenic gauntlets (BS EN 511) when filling LN₂ cold traps.
  • • Footwear: Enclosed, non-slip laboratory footwear worn at all times inside the instrument bay.

1. Physical Units: Why V/ms?

In Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometry (EA-IRMS or GC-C-IRMS), ion beams are collected in Faraday cups and converted into voltage signals (Volts, V) over retention time. Integrating beam intensity across peak elution yields physical peak areas reported in V/ms (or V·s). Each run provides a Reference Gas standard pulse with a known integrated area (e.g., 40.0 V/ms).

2. Visual Area Estimation: Height × Width

Chromatographic peaks are not simple rectangles. For a Gaussian or tailed peak:

Integrated Area (V/ms) ≈ Peak Height (V) × Width at Half Maximum (FWHM) × 1.064

Crucial Tip: A sharp analyte peak that is 2× taller than the reference pulse but half as wide has the exact same area in V/ms. Always inspect both peak crest voltage and base dispersion (τ)!
